120+ permit packages, without first-submission churn.

A commercial solar EPC needed dependable permit production across California AHJs while its internal engineers stayed focused on design exceptions and utility coordination.

Project Snapshot

Client: commercial solar EPC, anonymized. Scope: rooftop PV layouts, single-line diagrams, equipment schedules, code notes, structural coordination inputs, utility interconnection support, and AHJ-specific permit packages across California. Result: 120+ packages over 18 months with no first-submission rejections.

Client type

A commercial solar EPC selling and installing commercial rooftop PV systems across California. Its internal engineers handled design exceptions and utility coordination; Advanced Mechanix ran permit-package production as a managed service. The EPC is not named because the work was delivered under NDA.

Scope

  • Rooftop PV layouts and electrical single-line diagrams.
  • Equipment schedules and code notes.
  • Structural coordination inputs.
  • Utility interconnection support, including full interconnection applications.
  • Complete, AHJ-specific permit packages for each project.

Constraints

The client had sold more work than its drafting bench could comfortably absorb. The bottleneck was not one complex megaproject; it was the compounding effect of dozens of smaller commercial permits, each with its own AHJ preferences, utility forms and revision expectations.

Hiring internally would not solve the immediate queue. Sending work to generic drafting vendors had already produced inconsistent packages and avoidable correction cycles.

Standards

Every package was prepared to NEC 2023 Article 690 and to the requirements of the AHJ and utility involved. Those local requirements were captured in a permit checklist keyed by AHJ, utility, project size, equipment family and mounting type, and the client’s title blocks, notes, symbol usage and drawing order were standardised across the whole permit set.

Deliverables

  • 120+ commercial rooftop permit packages across California AHJs.
  • Utility interconnection applications for each package.
  • A permit checklist keyed by AHJ, utility, project size, equipment family and mounting type.
  • A standard permit-set structure: title blocks, notes, symbols and drawing order.
  • A comment log that fed reviewer and utility feedback back into the checklist.

Timeline

The managed service ran for 18 months. Packages were processed in weekly batches, with exception calls for unusual interconnection or structural conditions, and the average turnaround was five days per package. Permit comments were tracked and fed back into the checklist before the next batch, so the process improved as it ran; by month six the program was noticeably less dependent on memory and individual drafter preference.

Outcome

The engagement stabilised permit output without diluting engineering oversight. The EPC could keep selling and installing while Advanced Mechanix handled drawing production, package consistency and AHJ learning loops.

What made it durable

The checklist became more valuable over time. Each reviewer comment, utility preference and AHJ note was captured as structured operating knowledge. By month six, the program was not just faster; it was less dependent on memory and individual drafter preference.
