§01 — Why Houston

Gulf Coast engineering runs on drafting capacity you can't hire fast enough.

Houston is the operational centre for the world's largest concentration of oil & gas, petrochemical, and LNG engineering. Gulf Coast refineries, Permian infrastructure, LNG export terminals, Houston petrochemical corridor — every project runs on tens of thousands of sheets of electrical documentation that have to be produced, revised, and re-issued without a single hazardous-area classification error.

The bottleneck isn't engineering talent. Houston has that in depth. The bottleneck is specialist production drafting capacity when projects surge, and the rework cost when inexperienced vendors miss NEC Article 500 classifications or mis-route heat tracing.

We built Advanced Mechanix for exactly that problem. Our production team works to NEC 2023, API RP 500/505 hazardous-area classifications, and IEEE 515 heat-tracing standards, inside AutoCAD Electrical, Microstation, or client-hosted SmartPlant environments.

Quality: who checks the drawings?

The checking brief covers the project standard, drawing references and review responsibilities. We agree the internal checks, client review sequence, revision allowance and response windows as part of the package scope.

Standards: will you draw to our codes and templates?

We work to the standard that applies to your project: NEC (US), IEC 60364 and IEC 61439, AS/NZS 3000 (Australia and New Zealand), CSA C22.1 (Canada) and BS 7671 (UK). We draw inside your title blocks, layer conventions, cell and block libraries, not ours.

IP and NDA: what happens to our files?

Confidentiality and file access are agreed before source material is shared. Tell us if you require an NDA or a client-managed workspace, VDI or VPN. The scope identifies the permitted environment, access arrangements and handover requirements.

Stamps: who signs the drawings?

If your package requires a PE, Chartered Engineer or other statutory sign-off, tell us during scoping. We agree the responsible party and review requirements before production starts.

Time zones: when can we talk to the team?

Our production team is based in Pakistan (PKT, UTC+5). We agree your project’s working hours and handover arrangements during scoping. The table below uses a 09:00–18:00 PKT reference window to help plan the overlap; it is not a committed project schedule.

§04 — Houston-Specific FAQ

Questions Gulf Coast engineering managers ask us

Do you work to NEC 2023 and Texas state amendments?
Yes. Every US engagement is delivered to NEC 2023, plus applicable state and local amendments. Texas adopts NEC on a statewide basis, but Houston-area municipalities and counties may have additional amendments; we verify per-project.
Do you handle Class I Division 1/2 hazardous-area classification?
Yes. NEC Article 500 and 505 classification drawings, equipment schedules, and cable & raceway schedules for Class I and Class II hazardous locations. We reference API RP 500 / 505 for petroleum-specific classification and coordinate with your HAZOP outputs.
Can you support Gulf Coast EPC subcontractor workflows?
Yes. Most Houston EPC engagements run as subcontractor arrangements — we act as production drafting inside your title block, invisible to your end client. Standard flow-down NDAs, IP clauses, and deliverable ownership apply.
Can you produce heat-tracing documentation per IEEE 515?
Yes. Heat-tracing layouts, circuit schedules, temperature-maintenance calculations, and control documentation for pipeline freeze protection and process heating per IEEE 515. We support both self-regulating and constant-wattage systems.
What is your turnaround for CST-based clients?
The source information, package complexity and review requirements determine the scope. We agree the delivery programme, revision allowance and any specialist or statutory review responsibilities before production starts.
