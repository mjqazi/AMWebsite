What is a panel schedule?

A panel schedule is the table that describes every circuit in a panelboard or distribution board: what each circuit feeds, the protective device that protects it, the conductor or cable that carries it, and how much load it puts on each phase. It sits alongside the single-line diagram and the cable schedule, and it is usually printed and fixed inside the board door, so electricians and maintainers rely on it long after construction.

A good schedule lets an installer wire the board without guessing, lets an engineer see at a glance whether the phases are balanced and the board has spare capacity, and gives the next person to modify the board an accurate record to work from.

Two versions: NEC panelboard and AS/NZS distribution board

US and Australian practice describe the same thing in different terms, so the workbook keeps them on separate sheets rather than forcing one layout on both.

Which standards apply

  • United States: panelboards, load calculations and overcurrent protection follow the NEC (NFPA 70) edition your authority having jurisdiction has adopted, mainly the articles covering branch-circuit and feeder load calculations and panelboards.
  • Australia / New Zealand: the wiring rules are AS/NZS 3000, which also sets out how maximum demand is assessed; cable selection is to AS/NZS 3008.1.1; and the switchboard assembly itself is designed and verified to the AS/NZS 61439 series.

The workbook does not reproduce tables or demand factors from either standard. Enter values from your own licensed copy or your design software. The imbalance limit is a cell you set for your project; it is not a code value.

How to use the template

  1. Pick the sheet for your project: NEC Panel Schedule or AS-NZS DB Schedule.
  2. Fill in the header and system data. Yellow cells are example values; replace them.
  3. Add one row per circuit and delete or overwrite the highlighted EXAMPLE row.
  4. Enter the load on each phase. Totals, current per phase and the imbalance figure update automatically.
  5. Set your imbalance limit. The check cell shows OK or CHECK against it.
  6. AS/NZS sheet: enter the maximum demand from your AS/NZS 3000 assessment; the sheet compares it with the main switch rating.
  7. Keep revisions: update the Status column and the header revision every time the board changes.

The template is provided as-is. It is not engineering advice, and load calculations, device selection and cable sizing must be verified by a competent person against the applicable standard, the manufacturer’s data and the project specification.
