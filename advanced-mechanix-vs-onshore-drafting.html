In-house team or external design support?

The useful question is which parts of the industrial package need to stay close to your plant, and which can be produced against a clear brief.

Keep the design decisions close to the people who own them.

Site knowledge, operating constraints and changes to the design basis need a clear owner. For some projects, that is an in-house engineer. For others, it is a lead consultant or the engineer of record.

External support works best when the inputs and review responsibilities are explicit. It becomes harder when the drawing team is expected to discover undocumented site conditions or resolve design decisions without the right information.

Compare the whole package cost.

Look beyond an hourly rate. Include preparing the inputs, bringing the team onto your standards, review time, managing queries, revision allowances and handover. Ask suppliers to identify exclusions rather than assume that a low price covers them.

Keep it in-house when…

The work relies on frequent site decisions, immediate operational access or responsibilities that sit with your own engineering team.

Consider external support when…

The deliverables and inputs can be defined, the review owner is available and the package can move through an agreed issue process.

A practical mixed approach.

Your engineer owns the design basis and decisions. An external team develops and drafts the agreed SLDs, schedules, panel documentation or coordinated model. Queries and review comments return through a named contact.

Before appointing a team, agree the drawing brief , review allowance and source-file handover. Our cost comparison worksheet lets you use your own rates rather than assume an industry discount.
